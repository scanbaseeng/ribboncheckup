Short definitions, in the order you are likely to meet them. Each links to the Ribbon Checkup piece that goes deeper.

## Urine test strip terms

**Dipstick.** A plastic strip with chemical pads that change color in urine. The standard clinical version has ten pads. [What a 10 parameter dipstick measures](/health-explained/what-a-10-parameter-dipstick-measures/).

**Specific gravity.** The density of urine relative to water, a measure of concentration. Normal 1.005 to 1.030. [Specific gravity and hydration](/health-explained/specific-gravity-and-hydration/).

**pH.** Acidity of urine on a 0 to 14 scale. Normal 4.5 to 8. [Urine pH test strips](/health-explained/urine-ph-test-strips/).

**Protein (proteinuria).** Albumin leaking into urine. Trace is often harmless. Persistent 1+ or more is the classic early sign of kidney damage. [Protein in urine](/health-explained/protein-in-urine-proteinuria/).

**Glucose (glycosuria).** Sugar in urine, which appears when blood sugar exceeds roughly 180 mg/dL. [Glucose in urine](/health-explained/glucose-in-urine/).

**Ketones.** Byproducts of fat burning. The strip detects acetoacetate, not beta hydroxybutyrate. [Ketone test strips](/health-explained/ketone-test-strips/).

**Blood (hematuria).** Hemoglobin in urine. Can be red cells, free hemoglobin, or myoglobin. The pad cannot tell which.

**Nitrite.** Produced by many urinary bacteria from dietary nitrate. A positive pad strongly suggests infection. A negative does not exclude it. [Home UTI test kits](/guides/home-uti-test-kits/).

**Leukocyte esterase.** An enzyme from white blood cells. Positive with inflammation, usually infection. Reads at 60 to 120 seconds.

**Bilirubin and urobilinogen.** Pigments from red cell breakdown. Bilirubin in urine points to a liver or bile duct problem. Urobilinogen is normally present in small amounts.

**Microalbumin.** Albumin in urine at low concentrations that the standard protein pad misses. The early marker of kidney damage. [Kidney health urine tests](/health-explained/kidney-health-urine-tests/).

**uACR (urine albumin to creatinine ratio).** Albumin divided by creatinine in a single urine sample, reported in mg/g. Under 30 is normal. The test clinicians use to stage kidney disease.

**Midstream (clean catch).** A urine sample collected after the first part of the stream is discarded, to reduce contamination.

**First morning urine.** The first urine of the day. Most concentrated, and the standard sample for most strip tests.

## Blood test terms

**A1C (hemoglobin A1C).** The share of hemoglobin with glucose attached. Reflects average blood sugar over about three months. Under 5.7 percent is normal. [Diabetes screening](/preventive-health/diabetes-screening-a1c-and-fasting-glucose/).

**Fasting glucose.** Blood sugar after eight hours without food. Under 100 mg/dL is normal.

**Creatinine.** A muscle waste product cleared by the kidneys. Used to estimate kidney function.

**eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate).** An estimate of how much blood the kidneys filter per minute, calculated from creatinine, age, and sex. Under 60 for three months defines chronic kidney disease. [eGFR explained](/health-explained/egfr-explained/).

**ALT and AST.** Liver enzymes that rise when liver cells are injured. [Liver enzymes](/health-explained/liver-enzymes-alt-and-ast/).

**FIB-4.** A score calculated from age, ALT, AST, and platelets that estimates liver scarring risk. [The liver health checkup](/preventive-health/liver-health-checkup/).

**Lipid panel.** Total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides. [The cholesterol panel explained](/health-explained/cholesterol-panel-explained/).

**LDL and HDL.** Low density and high density lipoprotein. LDL deposits cholesterol in arteries and is the treatment target. HDL carries it away.

**Lipoprotein(a).** A genetic cholesterol particle that raises heart risk and does not respond to diet. Measured once in a lifetime.

**Comprehensive metabolic panel.** The standard blood panel of about 14 values including glucose, electrolytes, kidney markers, and liver enzymes.

## Screening and testing terms

**Sensitivity.** The share of people with a condition that a test correctly flags. [How to use home tests without fooling yourself](/articles/how-to-use-home-health-tests-without-fooling-yourself/).

**Specificity.** The share of people without a condition that a test correctly clears.

**False positive.** A positive result in someone who does not have the condition. Common when a condition is rare.

**False negative.** A negative result in someone who does have the condition.

**Base rate.** How common a condition is in the group being tested. It determines how much a positive result means.

**USPSTF.** The US Preventive Services Task Force, an independent panel that grades the evidence for screening tests. [Preventive screenings by decade](/preventive-health/preventive-screenings-by-decade/).

**FDA cleared.** A product reviewed by the FDA and permitted for sale for a stated use. For home tests, "over the counter" or "OTC" clearance is the relevant kind. [Where to buy urine test strips](/guides/where-to-buy-urine-test-strips/).

**CLIA waived.** A test classified as simple enough to run outside a formal laboratory. Applies to clinics and pharmacies, not home users.

**Research use only.** A product not cleared for diagnostic use. Sold without any performance claim.

**FIT.** Fecal immunochemical test. A home stool test for hidden blood, used for colorectal cancer screening. [Colorectal cancer screening options](/preventive-health/colorectal-cancer-screening-options/).

**Systolic and diastolic.** The top and bottom blood pressure numbers: pressure during a heartbeat and pressure between beats. [How to take blood pressure at home](/preventive-health/how-to-take-blood-pressure-at-home/).

**Validated device.** A blood pressure monitor tested against a reference standard under a published protocol. Lists are kept at validatebp.org.
