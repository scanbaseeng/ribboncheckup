Most people searching for a urine test kit want one of six things. Each has its own kind of kit. Buying the wrong one is the most common mistake, and it costs more than money. It gives you a result that answers a question you did not ask.

This guide sorts the kits by the question they answer. Then it lists what to check on the label before you buy.

## Match the kit to the question

| You want to know | Kit type | What it actually measures |
|---|---|---|
| Do I have a urinary tract infection? | Home UTI test | Nitrite and leukocyte esterase |
| Am I in ketosis? | Ketone strips | Acetoacetate in urine |
| Is my urine acidic or alkaline? | pH strips | Hydrogen ion concentration |
| Are my kidneys leaking protein? | Microalbumin or uACR test | Albumin, sometimes with creatinine |
| What is going on in general? | 10 parameter dipstick | Ten chemical markers at once |
| Am I pregnant? | hCG test | Human chorionic gonadotropin |

A standard 10 parameter dipstick covers the first four rows at once, but with less precision on each. It is the general screen. The single purpose kits are the focused tools.

Pregnancy tests and drug screening cups are their own categories. They use antibodies, not chemical pads. We cover them separately.

## The five things to check before you buy

### 1. Intended use

Look for the phrase "for in vitro diagnostic use" and, for home kits, "over the counter" or "OTC." Strips labeled "for research use only," "for veterinary use," or "for aquarium use" are not made or checked for human diagnosis. They are often cheaper. They are also a gamble. Some are fine. Some are the same chemistry with looser quality control. You cannot tell which from the listing.

### 2. Expiration date and lot

Strips have a shelf life, usually 18 to 24 months from manufacture. The reagent pads lose sensitivity as they age, and the failure is silent. An old strip does not show an error. It shows a weaker color and you read it as normal. Buy from sellers who show the expiration date. Avoid "bulk" listings with no date.

### 3. Quantity

Strips come in bottles of 25, 50, 100, or 150. Once you open the bottle, the clock starts. Most makers say to use them within 90 days of opening because humidity gets in each time you open the cap. If you plan to test twice a week, a bottle of 100 will expire before you finish it. Buy the count you will actually use.

### 4. Color chart quality

The chart on the bottle is the whole product. It is how you turn a color into a number. Cheap kits print charts with poor color separation, so trace and moderate look alike. Good kits print distinct blocks with a value under each one. If the seller shows the chart in the product photos, look at it. If they do not, that is a signal.

### 5. Parameters you will actually read

More pads is not better. A 14 parameter strip adds markers like ascorbic acid, calcium, or creatinine. Those are useful to a few people and noise to most. Every extra pad is one more color to match in a 60 second window. Buy the strip with the pads you need and no more.

## What no kit can do

A urine test kit screens. It does not diagnose. A positive nitrite result means bacteria are probably present. It does not tell you which bacteria or what to take for it. A negative result does not rule out infection. Roughly a third of UTIs are caused by organisms that do not produce nitrite.

Protein on a dipstick can mean kidney disease. It can also mean you exercised hard yesterday or have a fever today.

If you have symptoms, the kit can tell you whether it is worth a call. It cannot replace the call.

## Our short list by use

- **General screening at home:** a 10 parameter dipstick, 50 count, from a maker that lists an expiration date and shows the color chart.
- **UTI symptoms:** a two pad nitrite and leukocyte test. Test first morning urine. Read at exactly the time on the label.
- **Ketogenic diet:** ketone strips work well in the first weeks. After that, a blood ketone meter is more reliable. We explain why in the [ketone strips guide](/health-explained/ketone-test-strips/).
- **Kidney monitoring:** a microalbumin or albumin to creatinine ratio test, not a standard protein pad. The standard pad misses early leakage.
- **Diet and pH tracking:** plain pH strips with a 4.5 to 9 range. Do not pay for a 10 parameter strip to read one pad.

## Storage matters more than brand

Keep the bottle capped tight with the desiccant inside. Do not move strips into another container. Do not store them in the bathroom. Do not touch the pads. Most "bad batch" complaints in reviews are strips that were stored wet or warm. The brand did not fail. The bathroom did.
