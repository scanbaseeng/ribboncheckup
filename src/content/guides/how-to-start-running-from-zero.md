Running has the best return on time of any common exercise and the worst first month. About half of new runners are injured in their first year, and the great majority of those injuries come from the same cause: doing too much, too soon, at too high an effort. The fix is not talent or gear. It is patience for eight weeks.

## Before the first run

**Shoes.** Any running shoe from a running brand, in your size, with room for a thumb's width in front of the longest toe. Studies comparing shoe types find that comfort predicts injury better than any design feature. Do not buy on cushioning claims or pronation analysis.

**A baseline.** If you are over 45, have not exercised in years, or have heart disease, high blood pressure that is not controlled, or chest symptoms with exertion, see a clinician first. Ribbon Checkup's [annual physical guide](/preventive-health/annual-physical-what-should-happen/) covers what that visit should include. Most people do not need this step. The ones who do, know.

**Walking first.** If you cannot walk briskly for 30 minutes, start there. Two weeks of daily brisk walking is enough. See [why walking is the most underrated exercise](/articles/why-walking-is-the-most-underrated-exercise/).

## The two mistakes

**Mistake one: running too fast.** New runners run at a pace that leaves them gasping, then conclude that running is miserable. It is, at that pace. The pace to run at in the first two months is one where you can speak a full sentence. For most beginners that feels absurdly slow, slower than they imagine running should look. It is the correct pace. Elite runners spend about 80 percent of their training time at an easy, conversational effort.

**Mistake two: increasing too fast.** Cardiovascular fitness improves in weeks. Tendons, bones, and ligaments adapt over months. The gap between the two is where injuries happen: your lungs can do a 5K by week four and your shins cannot. Increase total weekly running time by no more than about 10 percent, and hold every other week flat.

## The eight week plan

Three sessions a week, on non consecutive days. Each session starts and ends with 5 minutes of walking. The intervals are run at a talking pace.

| Week | Session |
|---|---|
| 1 | Run 1 min, walk 2 min, x8 |
| 2 | Run 2 min, walk 2 min, x6 |
| 3 | Run 3 min, walk 2 min, x5 |
| 4 | Run 3 min, walk 1 min, x6 |
| 5 | Run 5 min, walk 2 min, x4 |
| 6 | Run 8 min, walk 2 min, x3 |
| 7 | Run 12 min, walk 2 min, x2 |
| 8 | Run 25 min continuous |

If a week feels hard, repeat it. There is no cost to taking ten weeks instead of eight. There is a cost to skipping ahead.

At the end, you can run for 25 minutes. That is roughly 3 to 4 kilometers for most beginners, and it is the base from which everything else is built.

## What normal discomfort feels like

Muscle soreness the day after a run, in the calves, thighs, and hips, is normal in the first weeks and fades. Breathlessness that resolves within a minute of stopping is normal. A side stitch is normal and goes away with slower breathing and a slower pace.

Not normal: pain in one specific spot on a bone, especially the shin or the top of the foot, that gets worse during a run rather than better. Pain that changes how you walk. Swelling. Knee pain that is sharp rather than achy. Any of these means stop for a week, then restart at a lower week of the plan. Bone pain that persists needs a clinician. Stress fractures are the injury that turns a hobby into a lost season.

## After week 8

Add time before adding speed. Take the 25 minute run to 30, then 35, across a month. Then make one of the three weekly runs longer, up to 45 or 60 minutes, still at talking pace. Only after two or three months of that does it make sense to add a faster session, and even then one per week is plenty.

Add strength. Two short sessions a week of squats, single leg work, and calf raises cuts running injury rates substantially. The [home strength program](/guides/a-beginner-strength-program-you-can-do-at-home/) covers it.

## Tracking it

A watch is optional. What is worth recording is the date, the minutes run, and how it felt on a scale of one to five. After two months, that log will show something a single run never does: that the same session that was a four in week three is a two in week eight. Fitness is invisible from the inside. It shows up in the record.
