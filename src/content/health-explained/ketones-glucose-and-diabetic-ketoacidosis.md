Most of what a urine test strip shows is a reason to think, repeat, or make an appointment. One pattern is different. A strongly positive ketone pad and a strongly positive glucose pad on the same strip, in someone who feels unwell, is a pattern that should send that person to urgent care or an emergency department the same day. This piece explains why.

## Two pads, two separate meanings

**Ketones alone** appear when the body burns fat for fuel. Fasting, a low carbohydrate diet, a stomach bug with vomiting, or hard endurance exercise all produce them. In a person without diabetes they are not dangerous. The body makes them, uses them, and keeps blood chemistry in balance. See [ketone test strips](/health-explained/ketone-test-strips/).

**Glucose alone** appears when blood sugar exceeds the kidney's threshold, roughly 180 mg/dL. It is a reason to check blood sugar and, if not already known, to be tested for diabetes. See [glucose in urine](/health-explained/glucose-in-urine/).

## What both together can mean

In diabetes, especially type 1, the body may lack enough insulin to move glucose into cells. Blood sugar rises, which puts glucose into urine. Cells, starved of glucose despite the excess in the blood, switch to burning fat, which makes ketones. Without insulin to regulate the process, ketones accumulate faster than the body can clear them, and the blood becomes acidic.

That is diabetic ketoacidosis, DKA. It develops over hours to a couple of days. It is treated in hospital with fluids and insulin. Untreated it is fatal. Treated early it is usually resolved in a day or two.

The urine strip pattern is glucose high, often 500 mg/dL or more, and ketones moderate to large. Add the symptoms below and the picture is clear.

## The symptoms that complete the picture

- Thirst that will not go away and frequent urination
- Nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain
- Deep, rapid breathing
- Breath that smells fruity or like nail polish remover
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty staying awake
- Weakness or feeling very unwell without an obvious reason

Any of these with the strip pattern above is a same day emergency. Do not wait to repeat the strip. Do not wait to see whether it improves overnight.

## Who is at risk

**Anyone with type 1 diabetes.** DKA is the classic presentation of new type 1, often in children and young adults who did not know they had it. It also happens in known type 1 when insulin is missed, an insulin pump fails, or an illness raises insulin needs.

**People with type 2 diabetes during illness.** Less common, but infections, heart attacks, and other stresses can trigger it.

**People taking SGLT2 inhibitors.** These diabetes drugs make the kidneys excrete glucose. They can cause a form of DKA where blood sugar is only mildly raised, sometimes under 250 mg/dL, which makes it easy to miss. Ketones with symptoms in someone on an SGLT2 inhibitor is an emergency even if glucose looks moderate.

**People on very low carbohydrate diets who also have diabetes.** The diet raises ketones. Diabetes disrupts the ability to keep them in check. The combination needs a clinician's supervision.

## What a urine strip cannot do here

It cannot measure blood ketones, which rise earlier and matter more. Urine ketone strips detect acetoacetate. In DKA the dominant ketone is beta hydroxybutyrate, which the strip does not see. A urine strip can underestimate DKA in its early hours.

That is why people with type 1 diabetes are advised to keep a blood ketone meter at home and to check blood ketones whenever blood sugar is over about 250 mg/dL or they feel unwell. A blood ketone reading above 1.5 mmol/L with high blood sugar is the threshold to call. Above 3.0 is an emergency.

A urine strip is a fallback. A positive result on it should be believed. A negative result, with symptoms, should not be trusted.

## What is not DKA

Ketones alone in a person without diabetes who is fasting, dieting, or recovering from a stomach bug, with normal or low glucose on the strip and no fruity breath or confusion, is not DKA. It is the body doing what it does without carbohydrate. Rehydrate, eat, and the ketones clear.

Glucose alone with no ketones is not DKA. It is high blood sugar, which deserves attention on its own timeline.

Starvation ketosis during pregnancy, hyperemesis, or severe illness can be significant and merits a call, but it is a different mechanism from DKA and is handled differently.

## The one rule

If a urine strip shows both glucose and ketones and the person feels unwell, treat it as an emergency until a clinician says otherwise. It is one of the few urine strip results where the right response is a car, not a calendar.
