Kidney disease is quiet. It usually produces no symptoms until most of the function is gone. The earliest reliable warning is a small amount of albumin leaking into urine. Catching it early is the entire game. The wrong test misses it.

## Three levels of protein testing

**Standard dipstick protein pad.** Detects albumin at roughly 15 to 30 mg/dL and above. It reports trace, 1+, 2+, 3+, 4+. Cheap and available on every 10 parameter strip. It misses low level leakage.

**Microalbumin strip.** A more sensitive pad tuned to albumin in the 10 to 20 mg/L range and up. "Micro" refers to the amount, not the size of the molecule. Catches leakage the standard pad calls negative.

**Albumin to creatinine ratio (uACR).** Measures albumin and creatinine together and reports the ratio in milligrams of albumin per gram of creatinine. Creatinine corrects for how dilute the urine is. This is the test clinicians actually use to stage kidney disease. It is available as a two pad home strip, as a mail in lab kit, and as a lab test ordered through a clinician.

## Why the ratio matters

Urine concentration swings all day. The same amount of albumin leaking per hour can read high in concentrated morning urine and low in dilute afternoon urine. A single albumin number is hard to interpret for that reason.

Creatinine is excreted at a fairly steady rate. Dividing albumin by creatinine cancels out the dilution. The result is stable enough to track over months.

The thresholds used in practice:

| uACR (mg/g) | Category |
|---|---|
| Under 30 | Normal to mildly increased |
| 30 to 300 | Moderately increased |
| Over 300 | Severely increased |

A single result over 30 is not a diagnosis. The standard is two out of three tests over 30, spaced across three months, to confirm persistent albuminuria. Exercise, fever, infection, and menstruation can all cause a temporary rise.

## Who should be testing

Guidelines recommend at least yearly albumin testing for people with diabetes, high blood pressure, or a family history of kidney disease. Those three groups account for most kidney disease in the United States. Many people in them have never had a urine albumin test.

For those people, a home uACR test is a reasonable way to check between clinic visits. It is not a replacement for the clinic test, which uses a calibrated analyzer. It is a way to find a problem sooner and bring a number to the visit.

For a healthy adult with none of those risk factors, routine home testing has limited value. A standard dipstick protein pad once in a while is enough. A persistent 1+ or higher is the signal to get a proper test.

## Reading a home uACR strip

Home uACR strips have two pads. One reads albumin. One reads creatinine. You match each to its own color scale, then find the ratio on a grid printed on the bottle or in the leaflet. Some kits use a phone app that reads the strip with the camera and does the math. The app versions remove the color matching step, which is where most errors happen.

Test first morning urine. It gives the most consistent ratio. Do not test within 24 hours of hard exercise or during a fever or urinary infection.

## What a result means

**Under 30, repeated:** no evidence of albumin leakage. Keep to the schedule your clinician sets.

**30 to 300, once:** repeat in a few weeks under good conditions. One elevated result is common and often means nothing.

**30 to 300, twice or more:** persistent. Bring the results to a clinician. This is the stage where treatment changes outcomes the most.

**Over 300:** call now. Do not wait to repeat.

**Standard dipstick protein 2+ or higher:** get a uACR test through a clinician. The dipstick has done its job.

## Buying notes

- A standard dipstick is not a kidney test. If the listing does not say microalbumin or albumin to creatinine ratio, it is the standard pad.
- Two pad uACR strips cost more than microalbumin only strips. The ratio is worth the difference.
- Kits sold as "kidney function" tests that only read protein and specific gravity are dipstick pads relabeled. The pads are fine. The claim is inflated.
- Mail in lab kits give a numeric result from a real analyzer. They cost the most and take days. For a one time check they are the most accurate home option.
- Check the expiration date. The albumin pad is one of the more sensitive to age.
